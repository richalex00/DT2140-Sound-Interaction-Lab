    Recipe reader: tilt the phone side to side

    Use case: your hands are covered in flour and you need the next step of the recipe without touching the screen.

    Mapping: tilt the phone to the right to go to the next step, to the left to go back, like turning the page of a cookbook. While you tilt you hear the paper rustle, more as the page lifts; at about 25° the page turns over with a swoosh towards that side (stereo). Then a marimba note confirms the step: the note goes up as you advance, so its pitch tells you how far you are in the recipe. The last step plays a short arpeggio. Bring the phone back level before the next page.

    Why: tilting is a coarse gesture you can do with your wrist, and the paper sounds make it feel like a real book: the rustle tells you the page is about to turn, the swoosh that it turned, and the rising note where you are. The sound is our own Faust model (faust-dsp/Kitchen/recipeBook.dsp): paper noise plus the course marimba model.
